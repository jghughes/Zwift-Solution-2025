1The determination of speed as a function of watts is based on the standard physics of cycling and takes into account various factors such as height, weight, wind resistance, rolling resistance, and gravitational forces. Height and weight are specific to each rider. Fixed coefficients for wind and rolling resistance are derived from experiments done by ZwiftInsider in August 2023 on a flat, timed section of Tempus Fugit. ZwiftInsider is 183cm, 75kg, 40-something, and Cat B.
2The power-saving benefits of drafting in the paceline are taken from the same experiments. ZwiftInsider tested a four-person paceline at 400W and 46kph on a standard Zwift TT bike with Zipp 808 wheels.
3Pull is the duration of each rider's pull at the front. For pace plans optimised by the algorithm, riders are ranked according to how hard they can pull for one-minute. Higher ranking riders are prioritised for longer pulls. The two highest ranking riders are assigned first and last place in the paceline-order, protecting weaker riders interleaved in the middle. Standard pull periods range from 30 seconds to four minutes. ZwiftPower graphs for each rider are mapped into best-fit curves for their anaerobic zone, their over/under zone, and their endurance zone respectively. A rider's over/under curve fits their 3.5 - 20 minute power graph on ZwiftPower. The over/under curve dominates calculations of pull power and duration. Over/under recovery ability is modelled on that of DaveK who is 182cm, 73kg, 30-something, and Cat B.
4zFTP is the acronym for Zwift Functional Threshold Power measured in Watts. For this app, ZwiftRacingApp is the source of zFTP, not ZwiftPower because the latter is sometimes unavailable. zFTP data and derived metrics are displayed for reference purposes only. They play no role in computations. Compared to ordering by descending zFTP, the pattern of interleaving gives faster and more balanced outcomes.
5NP is the acronym for Normalized Power in Watts. Calculated from rolling-average watts. A five-second rolling window is used.
as opposed to ranking by zFTP
6IF is the acronym for Intensity factor. IF is measured in terms of normalised power in watts divided by one-hour power in watts. One-hour power is taken from a rider's endurance curve. The endurance curve maps to a rider's ZwiftPower data in the 8 - 40-minute window. Sigma is the standard deviation of IFs for all riders. Smaller is superior.
7Limit indicates which metric for which rider constrains the speed of the paceline. The constraint protects riders from working beyond their limits, either during their pulls or overall. The speed of the paceline is governed by those who pull. The speed is held constant through the rotation. This means it is capped at the wattage ability and intensity factors of the weakest puller/s. Riders not assigned to pull are expected to just sit in and survive for as long as they can.
